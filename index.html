SLIIT Research Project · R26-IT-143

One camera. One sensor node. A safer, cleaner pool.

AquaSense is an AI-based smart swimming pool monitoring and maintenance system. It watches for drowning, litter, crowding and poor water in real time, combines the four signals in a central decision engine, and tells pool staff what needs attention first.

Project abstract

AquaSense in brief

Swimming pools are supervised, tested and cleaned by different people using different tools, and none of their observations are combined. AquaSense is an affordable AI system that uses a single camera and a single low-cost sensor node to monitor a pool continuously. Three computer-vision modules share the camera stream: crowd-aware maintenance scheduling, which turns bather counts into maintenance actions; drowning detection, which recognises the drowning posture with a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model; and garbage detection, which finds litter and estimates whether someone threw it. A fourth module classifies water quality from pH, temperature, turbidity and TDS readings using XGBoost.

A central decision engine fuses the four outputs into an overall risk level and a maintenance urgency level, shown on a real-time dashboard with alerts and a PDF session report. The drowning and litter detectors reached mAP@50 of 0.907 and 0.903, and the integrated system passed all 27 automated checks.

The problem

Pools are watched by many people, but never as one picture

Lifeguards watch swimmers, a technician tests the water a few times a day, cleaners work to a fixed calendar, and nobody records how busy the pool was. Drowning is silent and fast, water can degrade between tests, and a crowded holiday gets the same maintenance as a quiet weekday.

AquaSense brings these jobs together. Each module stays independent, but their outputs meet in a decision engine: a WARNING water reading during heavy use becomes more urgent, and a drowning alert in a crowded pool is flagged as harder to confirm visually.

Four components

What the system does

Each group member owns one research component. Together they form one product with one dashboard, one alert feed and one downloadable session report.

01Component 1

Crowd-aware maintenance

A person detector counts only the bathers inside the pool region, smooths the count, and turns it into bather load in person-hours. Load drives a ranked maintenance schedule and a capacity level (LOW to OVER CAPACITY).

M. H. M. Saajith · IT22156310
02Component 2

Water-quality monitoring

An Arduino node reads pH, temperature, turbidity and TDS with EEPROM-stored calibration. An XGBoost classifier rates the water SAFE, WARNING or CRITICAL, and offline sensors show as UNKNOWN rather than a false SAFE.

Yogarasa Nilogini · IT22247254
03Component 3

Drowning detection

A fine-tuned YOLOv8s model labels people as Swimming, Drowning or Person out of water. A temporal confirmation rule turns frame-level labels into a SAFE or DANGER state and pushes an instant alert.

N. M. Shafan · IT22248480
04Component 4

Garbage & littering intent

A YOLOv8n detector finds litter in the water while ignoring balls and leaves. A pose-based stage estimates whether a nearby person threw it, from arm raise, wrist speed and forward motion.

M. Y. S. Arsalan · IT22277336

Decision intelligence

From four alerts to one priority

  • Overall risk: NORMAL · ELEVATED · HIGH · EMERGENCY
  • Maintenance urgency: LOW · MEDIUM · HIGH · IMMEDIATE
  • Real-time dashboard over Flask-SocketIO, with live video and alert feed
  • One-click PDF session report for managers and inspectors
  • Designed as decision support for trained staff, not a replacement for lifeguards
